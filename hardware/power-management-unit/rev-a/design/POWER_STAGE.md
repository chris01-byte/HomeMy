# Revision A power-stage engineering record

Status: implementable prototype connectivity and bounded engineering calculations; not an energization or production release. All values are calculations or manufacturer specifications, not measurements of HomeMy hardware. `power-parts.json` is the component-by-component electrical record; `build_power_parts.py` reproduces it. `power_calculations.py` reproduces `../evidence/power/calculated.json` using Python 3 without third-party libraries.

The prototype retains the locked topology: external 60 A fuse, four-terminal main shunt, three MOSFETs in each main bank, system bus, two TPS26631 converter branches, and two MOSFETs in each motion bank with a separate four-terminal shunt. The analog brake remains on the motor side of the motion gate. No precharge or branch melting fuse is added.

## Circuit and package implementation

U1 drives common-source banks. Q1–Q3 have drains on `BATT_SENSED_P`, sources on `MAIN_COMMON`, gates individually connected through 10 Ω to HGATE. Q4–Q6 have sources on `MAIN_COMMON`, drains on `SYS_BUS_P`, and individual 10 Ω gates to DGATE. U1 A and OUT both sense the common-source copper; C senses the system output. MODE follows EN so reverse-current blocking stays enabled whenever powered. OVCLAMP is grounded; this is a disconnecting circuit breaker, not a linear surge regulator. The exposed pad **must remain floating**; neither a GND thermal connection nor a copper plane under that pad may silently change this instruction.

U1 VS is supplied from `BATT_SENSED_P` and decoupled by 100 nF. Its 1 µF CAP capacitor returns to VS, not ground. Every main MOSFET has a 10 MΩ gate-source resistor. A 100 kΩ substitution would load the 39 µA minimum HGATE source beyond its capability. The two voltage dividers sense the unswitched input, leaving SW unconnected. This prevents a divider powered from the common-source node from declaring startup UV before the first bank can turn on. Direct connections to VS and the sense inputs do **not** claim reverse-connected-battery tolerance. Keyed positive-polarity wiring is part of the prototype interface.

The shared main shunt is accepted as a design candidate with separate Kelvin branches: the INA228 uses its own pair of series filters; U1 CS+ uses R21 = 49.9 Ω, CS− goes directly to `MAIN_KELVIN_N`; ISCP has an independent 3.65 kΩ path from `MAIN_KELVIN_P`. Each differential filter returns to the negative Kelvin node. Main and INA series resistors must not be shared. A short or supply fault in either IC is not proven electrically independent merely because the traces are separate; failure injection remains required.

Q7/Q8 drains connect to `MOTION_SENSED_P` after RSH2, their sources join `MOTION_COMMON`; Q9/Q10 sources join that node and drains feed `MOTION_BUS_P`. U2 SRC returns to this same common-source node. PU reaches the common gate-drive node through R34 = 180 kΩ; PD reaches it directly, then each MOSFET has its own 10 Ω gate resistor. Thus the turn-on resistor does not slow fault pull-down. Each gate-source resistor is 10 MΩ (RC0805FR-0710ML, thick film, not the lower-range RT precision series). C9 and C29 are parallel 4.7 µF / 25 V X7R capacitors between BST and SRC. U2 OV is grounded deliberately: its native OV response automatically recovers without a dedicated fault flag. The independent 46 V chopper comparator and external motion latch provide the required latched OV inhibition. U2 EN/UVLO senses the system input; INP is the external permission-and-fault-latch output with a 100 kΩ default-low resistor.

RSH1/RSH2 use project-defined pad numbering: 1 left force, 2 left sense, 3 right sense, 4 right force. The physical manufacturer drawing has four distinct contacts. Current pads are 2.55 × 5.6 mm, at x = ±4.025 mm, y = +0.85 mm; sense pads are 2.55 × 0.8 mm, at x = ±4.025 mm, y = −3.25 mm. Kelvin pads must not merge into their adjacent force pours on the PCB. The Nexperia gate is physical pin 1, sources are pins 2–6, drains pins 7–12; library pad 13 represents the manufacturer drain mounting base `mb`. Package drawing p. 10, not a generic TO-Leadless footprint, controls the geometry.

U5 closes the native motion UV recovery path: TPS48110 EN/UVLO disables the gate during low system voltage but automatically recovers without asserting FLT_I. TLV3011B pin 1 joins `MOTION_FLT_N` as an open-drain wired OR; pin 2 is logic ground, pin 3 senses SYS through 226 kΩ / 10 kΩ, pins 4/5 join its 1.242 V reference, and pin 6 uses switched 3.3 V. Reference capacitance is 1 nF, supply decoupling 100 nF. The existing wake-sheet pullup and fault latch therefore capture both overcurrent and undervoltage. No input filter delays this comparison. U5 has 2–8 mV internal hysteresis. Its high-impedance POR interval is acceptable because motion begins unarmed.

The 27.89–30.73 V static engineering bound includes reference initial tolerance and 100 ppm/K over 100 K, 9 mV offset, the full 8 mV hysteresis as a conservative allowance, 2.5 mV common-mode and 1.5 mV supply-rejection allowances, and each divider resistor at 0.1% plus 25 ppm/K over 100 K. The lower threshold remains above native EN falling UV even after divider drift. This closes a sustained SYS-only dip/recovery path. TLV3011B specifies 4 µs maximum propagation at 100 mV input overdrive; it does not specify a universal minimum detectable pulse. Test shallow and fast SYS dips while 3.3 V stays alive, starting with pulses of at least 50 µs, and verify the external latch remains off after recovery. Do not describe arbitrarily short pulses as proven captured.

Q11 supplies the TPS remote temperature function, whose rising threshold is 150 °C typical and 140–160 °C in the current table. It is a final hardware backstop, not the desired board operating temperature. The separate motion-board NTC and external shutdown logic must act earlier.

Main UV is intentionally the native fast protection in this prototype. The requested 0.5 s UV delay is not supplied by TMR and is not claimed. The lower trip corner remains above the nominal 27.5 V BMS cutoff; a fast trip is a conservative, documented revision of this **provisional** setting. Similarly, 44.52 V is the nominal main OV setting, not a guaranteed 44–45 V window. Precision external threshold logic would be a design extension if that entire interval is required.

At 65 A the 0.5 mΩ shared shunt develops 32.5 mV. The LM74930 CSA accuracy table is specified over 6–30 mV, and the OCP mirror/error correlation is not sufficiently specified to turn the nominal equation into a guaranteed 65 A interval. A fabricated precision bound is therefore not reported. Nominal settings are adjustable, and load sweeps must establish the actual current trip before full-current use. The motion and main ranges overlap; neither resistor values nor their nominal ordering proves selectivity.

## Configurable hardware timing and latches

Both TMR pins use 33 µF film capacitors with 100 kΩ in parallel. The 100 kΩ prevents the low-current retry oscillator from reaching its upper threshold; toggling the documented enable/reset input clears the controller latch. The independent main and motion latches additionally capture voltage, temperature and external inhibition faults as described on the wake sheet. Main reset is a deliberate button cycle; motion reset is an explicit ESP reset with permission still gated. The TPS48110 thermal function otherwise retries after approximately 512 ms, so FLT_T must enter the external latch.

The exact first-trip expression is `t = -R C ln(1 - Vthreshold / [R (Isource - Ileak)])`, starting from discharged TMR. Use 65/85/97 µA for main source current and 73/82/91 µA for motion; the FLT warning and gate-off thresholds differ. With 10% capacitor tolerance, an additional ±2% thermal capacitance allowance, 0.1% resistor and 0–1 µA combined capacitor/PCB leakage, the calculated intervals are:

| Event | Nominal | Conditional interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Main FLT asserted; external latch disconnects | 0.457 s | 0.330–0.769 s |
| Main native gate turn-off | 0.502 s | 0.350–0.914 s |
| Motion FLT asserted; external latch disconnects | 0.475 s | 0.350–0.675 s |
| Motion native gate turn-off | 0.522 s | 0.379–0.737 s |

These are worst corners of the stated model, not guaranteed −40 to +125 °C limits. The capacitor manufacturer specifies insulation at 20 °C and publishes temperature curves as typical. The prototype local timer environment is assumed 0–60 °C and leakage ≤1 µA; verify those assumptions during bench characterization. Do not label the assembly “0.500 s ±10%.” A fault latch responding to FLT makes the FLT row the applicable removal time.

R22 = 18.2 kΩ and R64 = 200 Ω form the 18.4 kΩ ILIM setting using catalog resistor values. R31 = 39.7 kΩ is an actual catalog RT0805BRD0739K7L order code, also used by TI in TIDA-00929; it is not inferred solely from E-series arithmetic. U5 R62 uses catalog 226 kΩ.

## Gate drive, SOA and thermal basis

The selected MOSFET has explicit maximum RDS(on) of 0.99 mΩ at 25 °C, 1.6 mΩ at 100 °C and 2.3 mΩ at 175 °C with a 10 V gate. This gives useful hot data without promoting a typical temperature curve to a guarantee. The LM74930 DGATE minimum is 9.2 V, however. Its whole output bank therefore does not automatically inherit the 10 V hot table. Preliminary thermal sizing also evaluates 3.2 mΩ per die as a conservative engineering assumption; a hot measured gate/resistance result or further manufacturer bound is needed to close MAIN-002 fully. The motion driver has an 11 V minimum charge-pump turn-on threshold and adequate reservoir design for the normal enhanced state.

At 45 A, the 2.3 mΩ calculation gives 3.105 W across six main FETs, 4.658 W across four motion FETs, and 1.013 W in each shunt. At 60 A it gives 5.52 W, 8.28 W and 1.80 W per shunt. At 45 A the 3.2 mΩ fallback raises the FET losses to 4.32 W main and 6.48 W motion. These calculations exclude copper, connectors and unequal sharing. Each die's maximum junction-to-base resistance is 0.1 K/W; the 29 K/W typical board value applies to a particular 25.4 mm square test copper area and cannot be multiplied into a shared board thermal proof. Lay out separate generous drain copper, equal source/force resistance, thermal sensors and insulated heat-spreader provision. Different drain potentials must not be shorted by a common bare heatsink.

QG maximum is 539 nC and QGD maximum is 160 nC per die. The three main gates take approximately 47 ms at the minimum HGATE source current after the specified gate-source resistor and leakage load. Typical Miller slew estimate is 3.79 ms; neither charge ratio is an assured startup waveform. The motion reservoir needs at least 2.156 µF effective capacitance for one volt droop on four maximum gate charges. The generated KEMET C1206C475K3RACTU sheet shows about 40% typical capacitance loss at 12 V. Two populated parts provide about 3.88 µF after 10% tolerance, 15% temperature and an extra 10% aging allowance. This is a model based on a typical bias curve, with margin; observe BST−SRC during tests. Charging the nominal 9.4 µF reservoir to 12 V at 80 µA takes about 1.41 s while INP remains low.

For the initial motorless test use a 1 A current-limited supply, 20–30 °C starting temperature, ≤42 V and ≤100 µF directly switched bus capacitance. External converter inputs enter this test only after their capacitance and startup behavior are bounded. Capacitor energy is 0.0882 J at 42 V. The conservative resistor-charging loss is the same energy, and no equal current sharing among parallel FETs is credited in linear mode. On the primary SOA plot p. 4, a single device at 42 V/1 A/100 ms lies below the 25 °C mounting-base curve. This supports the bounded initial test only. A 25 °C graph is not a hot-battery-inrush proof; actual VDS, gate, current and temperature waveforms must be overlaid on a temperature-derated envelope for the full converter/motor system.

The chopper sheet adds two 470 µF / 100 V capacitors on MOTION_BUS: 940 µF nominal, 752–1128 µF including initial tolerance. Their 42 V energy is 0.82908 J nominal and 0.994896 J at the high capacitance corner. They are isolated during the initial main-switch fixture test. A 1 A supply is not a guaranteed functional motion-start source: inrush can cause SYS UV or main UV to latch. First characterize the analog chopper with the motion gate off and a separate current-limited fixture charging its capacitors. Controlled power-path inrush follows review of the captured VDS, ID, VGS and SYS voltage, using a bounded higher-current fixture as required; motor/battery operation remains disabled until that review.

R34 = 180 kΩ and the four 10 MΩ gate-source bleeds are the initial motion slew setting for this added capacitance. Only the two input-bank FETs are credited with Miller charge: the output bank can follow its body diode, so four QGD must not be summed to predict a slower ramp. With two typical 69.5 nC gate-drain charges, the 4.3 V typical plateau, 12 V boost and bleed/leakage allowance, the estimate is 3.42 ms and 11.55 A average at nominal capacitance. Using two 21 nC minimum table charges, an assumed 2 V plateau and 13 V boost estimates 0.687 ms and 68.93 A at maximum capacitance. **The gate-charge table applies at 50 V / 25 A / 25 °C: these estimates are not guaranteed cold/hot 42 V inrush bounds.** A complete SOA proof cannot be obtained by multiplying average current by a typical ramp. Steady gate drive with an 11 V boost minimum is approximately 10.19 V nominal and 10.16 V with +2% turn-on resistance / −3% bleed resistance allowance. Direct PD discharge bypasses R34, preserving rapid fault removal.

Controller response time alone is not the switch turn-off time. The main minimum 128 mA sink requires approximately 12.6 µs to remove three maximum total gate charges, before external sensing/filter delays are considered. Each 1 nF ISCP capacitor and its programming resistor also add an input-dependent response. For a preliminary stress check, 42 V × 120 A × 25 µs = 0.126 J; this 25 µs is an engineering allowance, not a proved maximum. Input wiring at an assumed 2 µH stores 0.0144 J at 120 A. Clamp that energy locally; do not assign the MOSFET's single-pulse avalanche rating as a repetitive operational budget.

## Shunt energy and converter branches

Each shunt dissipates 1.25 W at 50 A and 2.113 W at 65 A. The 150 A, 3 s architecture pulse is 11.25 W and 33.75 J. The selected shunt's specified short-time overload test is five times its 10 W rating for five seconds: 50 W for 5 s, or 250 J under that qualification. This is stronger evidence than a bare 10 W nameplate for the requested 34 J event, but it is not a universal energy rating independent of pulse shape or starting temperature. Keep the force terminals within their derated temperature envelope and measure resistance drift after approved pulses. INA228 full scale with 163.84 mV and 0.5 mΩ is 327.68 A; calibration must use the actual shunt resistance.

U3 PC ILIM = 6.04 kΩ gives 2.980 A nominal. U4 logic ILIM = 9.09 kΩ gives 1.980 A. A conservative ±10% engineering envelope gives maxima about 3.28 and 2.18 A including resistor tolerance; the exact TPS26631 pulse-overcurrent and fast-trip behaviors also apply. The device permits overload pulses up to approximately twice the programmed level before its internal overload timing acts; a “3 A branch” is not a rigid 3 A clamp. Neither branch is the precision 65 W limiter. The PC's 65 W budget is 2.24 A at 29 V. A 25 W logic converter at 85% efficiency needs about 1.014 A at 29 V, leaving startup margin.

Both MODE pins float for latch-off. B_GATE and DRV float, IN and IN_SYS are joined, as documented when the external blocking FET is unused. This gives no separate converter-to-system reverse-current guarantee; the main ideal diode remains the battery barrier. SHDN uses a 1 MΩ / 68 kΩ divider from SYS_BUS, so the logic branch can start before ESP power. It resets with a deliberate main cycle, avoiding a self-powered startup deadlock. Both dVdT capacitors are 22 nF C0G: about 18.48 ms to ramp 42 V, with a 15.17–23.26 ms component/table calculation. TPS thermal shutdown protects its die but is not a proof of converter or harness suitability.

Motion IMON uses a 10 kΩ load plus a 20 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider. The unloaded interface sensitivity is 11.25 mV/A. The wake sheet's additional 100 kΩ ADC pulldown changes the combined sensitivity to about 10.465 mV/A. Account for that loading in firmware calibration. Even 7.5 V at the raw IMON absolute limit produces at most 2.504 V on the isolated input with 0.1% resistor corners; normal IMON linear operation remains within the controller's stated range. A 10 nF local capacitor and the wake ADC capacitor filter noise without sharing current-threshold filters.

## Transient coordination and remaining design limits

D1 and D2 use the lower-clamp PTVS1-043C-H. Ordinary SMCJ43A would specify approximately 69.4 V at its rated pulse, leaving essentially no LM74930 70 V absolute-maximum margin and exceeding the normal TPS26631 67 V limit. The selected part publishes 48–53 V breakdown, 56 V **typical** clamp at 1 kA with an 8/20 µs pulse, and a 0.1%/°C breakdown coefficient. It is an implementable prototype selection with a much better typical envelope; its sheet does not provide a guaranteed maximum clamp over all relevant currents and temperatures. The same part must not be represented as a proved 56 V maximum clamp.

Keep TVS return loops separate from logic/Kelvin ground. Use a 60 V peak-at-controller design target, short low-inductance loops and voltage probes at the pins. The actual upper bound including temperature, device spread and L·di/dt remains to be established from a manufacturer maximum envelope or approved low-energy characterization. A 55 V claimed converter input rating also needs review: an eFuse OVP threshold followed by a microsecond turn-off delay does not prove a perfect 55 V output clamp. These are explicit design-review limitations, not completed measurements and not hidden production claims.

Before a battery is attached, close the repository's exact external-fuse and current-limited bring-up gates. Before high-current prototype tests, establish actual voltage/current trip behavior, main hot gate drive, transient bounds and the stated local timer assumptions. After build, the full no-precharge, connector/cable heating, regeneration and fuse/BMS selectivity measurements belong to Revision B validation. The design does not claim that the nominal 60/65 A settings are selective; it is acceptable for both paths to shut down during a fault, and neither may retry automatically.

The machine-readable `unresolved_design_items` intentionally distinguishes missing engineering bounds from later physical validation. No single clean ERC/DRC result can close these electrical limits.
